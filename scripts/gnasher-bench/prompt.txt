You are a careful senior engineer reviewing a design proposal. Read the proposal below and then answer the questions at the end in full sentences.

Proposal: We want to run a 320-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts language model on laptops with limited unified memory. The model has 45 layers; the first three are dense and the remaining 42 route each token through 8 of 288 experts plus one shared expert. Each routed expert holds about 25 million parameters, so at 4-bit precision one expert blob is roughly 14 megabytes and a single token touches 336 blobs, or about 4.7 gigabytes of expert weights. The non-expert core (attention, routers, shared experts, embeddings and head) is about 16 billion parameters and must stay resident, which costs 9 gigabytes at 4-bit or 17 gigabytes at 8-bit. The plan is to keep the core resident, stream routed experts from the SSD on demand into a bounded per-layer cache, and evict by a decaying hotness score. Prefill will be chunked so one fetched expert can serve many prompt rows. The KV cache is small because 34 of the layers use linear attention with constant-size state and the other 11 use compressed multi-head latent attention, about 11 kilobytes per token.

Open questions the team raised: (1) The SSD delivers about 5 gigabytes per second on large reads, so a cold token costs almost a second of I/O. How much cache hit rate is needed before decode exceeds 3 tokens per second? (2) Does pinning experts by low ID make sense, or should the entire cache be a hotness-ranked pool? (3) On an 8-bit core, what is the smallest laptop memory size that leaves at least 12 gigabytes for the expert cache after reserving 4 gigabytes for the operating system? (4) The macOS file cache will also hold recently read experts; why does that make measurements on a 128-gigabyte machine optimistic for a 32-gigabyte machine, and how would you control for it? (5) Suggest two experiments, each described in one paragraph, that would tell us whether routing is skewed enough for a small cache to work.

Answer all five questions. Show arithmetic where relevant. Be concrete and do not pad your answer.
